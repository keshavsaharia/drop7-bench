Drop7 is played on a 7×7 board. You drop one numbered disc at a time into a
column, and it falls to the lowest empty cell. The number on a disc is a rule
about **group size**: a disc disappears when the unbroken horizontal or
vertical group it sits in has a length exactly equal to its number. Gray discs
count as occupied cells, so a group that touches a gray disc is longer than it
looks.

## A disc clears when its group matches its number

Here the bottom row holds two 3s with empty cells on both sides. Dropping a
third <Disc n={3} /> in column 5 completes a group of exactly three, so all
three discs clear — and the gray disc resting on them takes a hit, cracks, and
drops:

## Clears cascade into chain reactions

## Gray discs hide fixed numbers

A solid gray disc <Disc solid /> hides a number. It takes **two** neighboring
clears to open: the first hit cracks it <Disc cracked />, the second reveals
the number underneath. Here two 1s — each sitting in a group of one — pop and
their two hits open the gray disc between them:

<BoardCompare
  before="0000000000000000000000000000000000000010000008100"
  after="0000000000000000000000000000000000000000007005000"

  afterLabel="The gray disc reveals its 5"

In the scripted benchmark rounds used by the leaderboard, that hidden number
is **predetermined**: the 5 was fixed when the covered row appeared, and every
policy that opens this disc finds the same 5.

## Every five drops, a covered row rises

The rise clock ticks down from 5. When it reaches zero, the whole board rises
one row and a **new covered row** appears at the bottom, with a 17,000-point
level bonus:

<BoardCompare
  before="0000000000000000000000000000000000000040008888888"
  after="0000000000000000000000000000700400088888888888888"
  beforeLabel="The fifth drop lands"
  afterLabel="The board rises"
  caption="Everything shifts up one row and a fresh row of gray discs appears below. If any cell blocks the top row, the rise fails and the game ends."
/>

The game ends when a row cannot rise, or when no column can accept another
disc.

## Scoring rewards deep chains

Each disc cleared in chain wave `d` awards `floor(7 × d^2.5)` points, so late
waves are worth far more than early ones:

| Chain wave | Points per disc | Feel |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 7 | routine clear |
| 2 | 39 | nice follow-up |
| 3 | 109 | real chain |
| 4 | 224 | big cascade |
| 6 | 617 | spectacular |
| 8 | 1,267 | board-shaking |

Clearing the entire board awards **70,000** points, and each row rise awards
**17,000** points. This is the corrected five-move Hardcore scoring used
throughout the repository (historical runs with a 7,000-point level bonus are
archival only).

## Why the strategy is hard

A move is never just its immediate clear: it changes which gray discs get
cracked, which numbers fall together, and what the board looks like after the
next rise. The research question — which column should receive the next disc,
using only what a player can see — is what every approach in this repository
tries to answer.

<Callout title="The public information boundary" tone="info">
A legal policy may use only the visible board, the visible next disc, the
moves until the next rise, and whether the game is over. Never the seed,
future discs, hidden gray-disc values, the score, or the level.
</Callout>
