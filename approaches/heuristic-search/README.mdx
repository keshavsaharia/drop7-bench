The oldest way to play: score what you can see. Height, holes, exposed
numbers, reachable triggers, covered-disc access — a weighted sum of visible
features, optionally wrapped in one or two plies of lookahead or a short
sampled rollout.

## Why it matters

Heuristics are the **leaves** of every deeper search and the **continuation
policies** inside every rollout. When a learned or searched policy fails, the
post-mortem usually ends here: the features mis-valued a board that looked
fine. The research surfaced one durable diagnostic — sustainable long games
keep roughly **2.4 numbered clears and 1.4 covered reveals per move** — and
several policies here (gray-throughput, phase-horizon) optimize that flow
directly.

## What the evidence showed

- Immediate score is a poor guide: a policy must keep opening covered numbers
  and preserve future chain structure.
- Hand-tuned evolutions of the feature weights failed their whole-game gates
  even when the feature directions looked plausible.
- These policies are cheap and deterministic, which is why several of them
  (greedy, gray-throughput, rollout, risk-sensitive, open-loop beam) power the
  scripted-round leaderboard as reference points.
