N-tuple learning values a board by summing many small lookup tables, each
indexing a handful of cells (a "tuple"). It is the classic approach for games
like 2048, and it was the **first learned-policy line** tried here: a
hierarchical 5.45-million-weight model trained over half a million games, then
a long series of TD, Q-learning, and PPO variants.

## What the evidence showed

- Sparse learned-value search over the n-tuple improved the learned greedy
  baseline — real progress — but was not selected over the later fair-D4
  reference.
- The RL variants (rainbow Q, native and torch PPO, primal-dual actor-critic)
  repeatedly plateaued below fair search or failed their Stage-A floors.
- A subtle, instructive bug class lives here: shared-parameter TD updates
  needed temporal-coherence corrections, and even after the fix the policy
  gates did not pass.
