Where expectimax enumerates chance exactly, Monte Carlo tree search **samples**
it: play many randomized futures, remember which root actions do well, and
concentrate sampling there.

## The Drop7 twist

Drop7's chance nodes break the usual MCTS assumptions. The repository's
observable-state variant samples chance at the edges where it actually
happens, avoiding "strategy fusion" (planning as if hidden information will be
known later). Even so, the ledger verdicts were consistent: ordinary MCTS did
not establish whole-game improvement, confidence-gated overrides worsened
ranking, and scaling simulations made long-outcome ranking **worse**, not
better. The diagnosed bottleneck is the weak continuation policy inside the
rollouts — replace the continuation, not the budget.

A bounded TypeScript MCTS is in the benchmark registry, so you can see its
behavior on the [leaderboard](/leaderboard).
