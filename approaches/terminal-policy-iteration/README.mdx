---
title: Terminal policy iteration
summary: Judge actions by how whole games actually end, and override the baseline only with statistical confidence.
---

Instead of trusting a heuristic leaf, run each candidate root action through
many long continuations — hundreds of moves, sometimes to the game's end — and
compare the distributions. Then, crucially, only override the baseline policy
when the improvement is statistically supported; otherwise keep the safe
choice.

## Why it matters

This family produced the repository's most careful **offline** evaluation
asset: a locked panel of 477 public roots with every legal sibling labeled by
long continuations. On that panel the latest completed work found:

- a martingale-dual ranker underperformed fair D4 (28.93% vs 38.16% top-1
  accuracy);
- a regenerative policy-iteration variant was nearly identical to D4,
  overriding only 11 roots — six helped, but confidence bounds were negative.

Neither justified a gameplay run, and both were recorded as valid negatives.
The lesson generalizes: **conservative overrides need overwhelming evidence**,
because a wrong override costs more than a right one gains.
