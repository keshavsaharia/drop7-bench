An oracle plays with the future tape visible. That is cheating as a policy —
but a legitimate way to answer two questions: **how much can structure matter**
(a ceiling), and **can a public student be taught** (distillation).

## What the evidence showed

- The perfect-information oracle demonstrated that million-point structure
  **exists** in the simulator — an upper-bound teaching signal, never a legal
  policy result.
- Distillation kept failing the same way: students drifted, held-out labels
  overfit, and apparent screen gains reversed in confirmation. Oracle choices
  are often simply not recoverable from the public state.
- The topology audits did identify split-stable observable geometry — the kind
  of signal that survives the privilege boundary.

<Callout title="Never compare scores across the boundary" tone="warn">
Oracle scores use future information and must never be placed on the same
scale as public-information policies.
</Callout>
